Too Many Cooks

Marketplace + Chef Profile Blueprint

A practical product and design brief for everything after the customer enters the marketplace: discovery, trust, ordering, and the chef workspace behind it.

Customers arrive hungry. They discover a meal first, then the cook and story behind it.
Product direction brief · September 2026
Based on the current Expo + Supabase app and the marketplace brainstorm
01 · North Star

What the customer experience should do

The marketplace should help a customer go from “I want something good” to “I know what I’m ordering, who made it, and how I’ll get it” with as little friction as possible.

Core principle: meals lead the page; cook identity and trust information travel with every meal.

Customer default

Customer login opens the marketplace at /discover, with search ready immediately.

Chef default

Chef login opens the existing menu workspace at /(cook)/meals. The dashboard remains available, but is secondary.

Customer navigation

Market, Saved, Orders, Account.

Chef navigation

My Menu, Orders, Account. Market and Saved stay customer-only.

Success looks like

  • A customer can search by meal, cook, cuisine, or craving.
  • A customer can understand availability, price, pickup/delivery, and next action from a card.
  • A customer can trust a cook through identity, location, cuisine, ratings, and profile story.
  • A chef can publish a dish, manage availability, view orders, and maintain a credible profile.
  • A user with both roles can choose Customer or Chef after sign-in.
02 · Marketplace

The customer page

Make the first screen feel like a lively local food market rather than a database browser.

First viewport

  1. Warm discovery header: “What are you craving today?”
  2. Primary search: “Search dishes, cooks, or cuisines.”
  3. Location: a visible city/location selector such as Philadelphia.
  4. Availability signal: “12 cooks nearby” or the current live count.
  5. Quick discovery chips: Comfort food, Weeknight dinner, Vegetarian, Desserts, Meal prep, New this week.

Mobile order

1. Search and location

Search is the first action. Location sets realistic results.

2. Browse by craving

Chips make discovery possible when the customer has a mood, not a dish name.

3. Popular near you

Lead with meals that are available now and close enough to fulfill.

4. Cooks you may like

Introduce personality and encourage following.

5. Fresh from the kitchen

Newly published dishes create a reason to return.

6. Followed cooks

Personalized repeat behavior for customers who come back.

Desktop order

Use a compact filter rail on the left and a meal-first discovery grid on the right. Keep cook details attached to each meal. Move full cook browsing to a dedicated cook profile route instead of making the main page behave like a split database panel.

03 · Search + Discovery

Make search feel forgiving

The current search model already supports meal names, cook names, cuisines, categories, and distributed matches. The UI should make that power legible.

Customer intentSearch exampleResponse
Specific dish“jollof rice”Show matching meals and their cooks.
Cuisine“Indian food”Normalize “food” and match cuisine/category terms.
Mood or need“spicy dinner”Match categories, descriptions, and craving chips.
Person“Amara”Show the cook profile and current menu.

Controls

Use friendly language: Available now instead of “In stock only.” Allow a clear “Include sold out” alternative. Keep refresh visible but secondary. Show the active query as a removable filter.

Empty, loading, and error states

Empty

“No cooks found for ‘ramen’ yet. Try tacos, vegetarian, dessert, or meal prep.”

Loading

Use skeleton meal and cook cards rather than a single “Loading marketplace...” line.

Offline/API error

“We couldn’t refresh the market. Your last results are still here.” Offer retry.

Preview data

Keep demo data useful in development, but label it clearly and never present it as live inventory.

04 · Meal Cards

The conversion surface

Every meal card should answer the customer’s practical questions before they tap.

Card anatomy: real food image · meal name · cook name · location · short description · price · availability · fulfillment timing · primary action.

What to build next

  • Use real meal photography as the dominant visual whenever a photo exists.
  • Add a clear “View meal” action that leads to the existing meal detail and checkout path.
  • Expose pickup, cook delivery, or platform delivery in plain language.
  • Show quantity and timing without making the card feel like inventory software.
  • Keep follow and full-menu actions on the cook surface, not competing with the meal purchase action.
05 · Cook Identity

The chef profile

The chef profile is the trust layer. Customers are choosing a kitchen, not only a menu item.

Profile header

Identity

Cook name, profile image or kitchen image, verification badge, rating, review count.

Place

City or neighborhood, fulfillment radius, pickup/delivery options.

Point of view

Short bio, cuisines, cooking style, and what makes this kitchen distinct.

Proof

Completed orders, repeat customer rate, food-safety badge, and transparent new-cook status.

Profile actions

View menu should be the primary action. Follow cook is secondary and should update immediately. A customer should also be able to browse the cook’s public menu, view a meal, and return to the marketplace without losing their search context.

Voice

Keep the writing human and specific. “Small-batch meals with big Sunday-dinner energy” is more persuasive than “high-quality food service.”

06 · Chef Workspace

The chef profile and menu builder

Chef login should open My Menu. The existing dashboard remains useful, but the chef’s first job is to make something customers can buy.

Chef navigation

My Menu

Create meals, edit drafts, upload photos, set prices and quantities, publish or hide items, view public menu, share menu link.

Orders

See new orders, accept or decline, manage ready/completed status, and view customer/order details.

Account

Chef profile, kitchen identity, city, bio, cuisines, availability, verification, legal/compliance details.

Menu item creation

  1. Title and food photo
  2. Description that makes the dish sound good
  3. Food categories and cuisine
  4. Price and quantity available
  5. Service type: prepared meal, meal prep, or in-home chef
  6. Fulfillment: pickup, cook delivery, or platform delivery
  7. Availability window and lead time
  8. Draft, preview, publish, and hide states

Chef profile completion

  • Display name and kitchen name
  • Bio, cuisine tags, cook type, and service area
  • Profile image or kitchen image
  • Availability and fulfillment settings
  • Food handler permit and compliance attestations where required
  • Public menu slug and shareable menu link
07 · Data + Routes

Functional contracts

FlowExpected destinationDefinition of done
Customer sign-in/(customer)/discoverMarketplace loads; search and live cooks/meals are usable.
Chef sign-in/(cook)/mealsMy Menu loads; chef can create, edit, publish, and share.
Dual-role sign-in/(auth)/choose-profileCustomer and Chef choices route independently.
Meal click/meals/[mealId]Customer sees detail, availability, and checkout action.
Cook click/cooks/[cookId] or public menuCustomer sees identity, trust, menu, and follow state.

Core data the marketplace needs

Cook: display name, bio, city, cuisines, cook types, verification, ratings, follower count, active state.

Meal: title, description, photo, price, quantity, categories, service type, fulfillment modes, published state, updated time, cook identity.

Customer state: follows, saved meals, recent searches, orders, selected location.

08 · Build Order

What to focus on next

1
Lock the role flows.
Customer → marketplace. Chef → My Menu. Dual role → chooser.
2
Make the marketplace meal-first.
Improve cards, images, availability, location, and the View meal action.
3
Finish the chef profile and menu loop.
A chef must be able to represent their kitchen, publish a dish, and see the public result.
4
Make search and empty states feel intentional.
Customers should never be confused by zero results, loading, sold-out items, or API errors.
5
Only then add polish.
Motion, typography, photography, refined spacing, and responsive desktop composition.

Acceptance checklist

  • Customer can search by dish, cuisine, cook, or craving.
  • Customer can filter available meals and understand fulfillment.
  • Customer can follow a cook and browse that cook’s menu.
  • Customer can open meal detail and continue toward checkout.
  • Chef can create and publish a meal with complete information.
  • Chef can edit, hide, and share a public menu.
  • Chef can view orders and update order status.
  • Customer and chef navigation never expose the wrong role’s primary tabs.
This blueprint is intentionally product-focused. It describes the experience to build on top of the existing Expo Router, Supabase Auth, meals, profiles, follows, orders, and storage foundations.
